Mentalink

A mental wellness concept app designed to help users track their emotional state, build self-awareness, and access supportive resources — from research to interactive prototype.

UX Research & UI Design

Course project period

Problem

Mental health apps often feel clinical or overwhelming. Users need a gentler, more personal way to track emotional wellbeing.

Goal

Design a calming, intuitive app concept that supports daily emotional check-ins and resource discovery.

My Role

End-to-end UX: user research, personas, information architecture, wireframing, UI design, and prototype.

Overview

Mentalink is a concept mobile application designed to support mental wellness through daily emotional check-ins, mood tracking, and curated resource recommendations. The project was completed as a UX design course assignment, covering the full design process from research to interactive prototype.

Problem & Context

Many existing mental health apps feel clinical, impersonal, or feature-heavy. Users who are beginning to pay attention to their mental wellbeing often feel overwhelmed by complex interfaces. The challenge was to create something gentle and approachable that encourages consistent daily use without pressure.

Research & Insights

Research included a user survey, competitive analysis of existing wellness apps, and secondary research on emotional tracking patterns. Key findings shaped the design direction.

  • Users prefer simple check-in flows that take under a minute
  • Warm colors and soft visuals reduce perceived pressure
  • Resource suggestions are valued when they feel relevant, not generic

Key insight: Users expressed that most mental health tools feel like "work." The biggest opportunity was to make the experience feel like a supportive conversation, not a clinical assessment.

Ideation & Decisions

I developed user personas, mapped user flows for the core daily check-in journey, and explored multiple wireframe layouts. Key decisions focused on minimizing cognitive load and maintaining a warm, encouraging tone throughout the interface.

  • One-tap mood selection as the primary interaction
  • Optional journaling to lower the barrier to entry
  • Contextual resource cards based on selected mood

Design Solution

The final design uses a calming color palette, rounded shapes, and a minimal layout. The core flow takes users from a daily mood check-in to personalized resource suggestions in just a few taps.

  • Home screen with gentle mood prompt
  • Mood history timeline with visual patterns
  • Curated resource cards that adapt to recent entries
  • Clean settings and profile without clutter

Validation & Iteration

Usability testing was conducted with five participants using the interactive Figma prototype. Participants completed core tasks while thinking aloud. Feedback was overwhelmingly positive regarding the tone and simplicity of the interface.

  • All participants completed the check-in flow without guidance
  • Two participants suggested more prominent access to history
  • Minor label adjustments were made based on feedback

Results & Learnings

The project demonstrated that a gentle, minimal approach to mental wellness design can feel both supportive and functional. The prototype received positive usability feedback and the process reinforced the value of starting with user emotions rather than features.

  • Validated that simplicity and warmth can be a differentiator in wellness apps
  • Learned the importance of tone in micro-copy for sensitive topics
  • Would explore notification patterns and long-term engagement in future iterations
