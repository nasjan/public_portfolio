Mentalink

Therapist Discovery Platform Concept. The idea was to combine the logic of Tinder with the process of finding a therapist.

UX Design and Concept

Finding a therapist feels overwhelming. Current services rely on long lists and complex filtering, creating a high barrier to start.

Goal

Overview

Mentalink was an innovation course project. I developed the concept together with Miikka Saukkonen in a two person team.

The idea was to combine the logic of Tinder with the process of finding a therapist. The goal was to make mental health services easier to approach, especially for young adults and working age users.

Many current services feel formal and difficult to navigate. Mentalink aimed to offer a modern and simple alternative.

View demo

Team and My Role

This project was completed in a two person team with Miikka Saukkonen.

We worked closely together in all stages of the project. My main focus was:

  • User experience structure
  • User flow planning
  • Interface clarity
  • Feature prioritization
  • Prototype design

I took special responsibility for defining how the user interacts with the service and how the experience should feel from start to finish.

The Problem

Finding a therapist can feel overwhelming.

Users often face:

  • Too many options
  • Complex websites
  • Unclear differences between professionals
  • High emotional barrier to start

Many services rely on long lists and filtering systems. This can feel impersonal and stressful. We wanted to reduce this barrier and create a more human experience.

The Idea

Mentalink uses a swipe style browsing system.

Users can:

  • Browse therapist profiles one by one
  • View clear and simple information
  • Save profiles they like
  • Contact professionals easily

The idea was to make the process feel natural and less bureaucratic.

The platform also includes a support section called Mentaspace. It offers articles and mental health resources for users who are not yet ready to start therapy.

Key Features

Onboarding

When users join the platform, they answer a few simple questions. This helps define their needs and preferences.

Swipe Browsing

Users browse therapists like a card deck. Each profile shows:

  • Specialization
  • Short bio
  • Location
  • Services offered

Users can save profiles for later review.

Saved Profiles

Saved therapists are stored in one place. Users can compare profiles or move forward with contact.

Profile Page

Each therapist profile includes:

  • Clear description
  • Human tone
  • Simple contact action

The aim was to reduce hesitation and make the next step feel safe.

Target Group

Mentalink focuses mainly on:

  • Young adults
  • Working age users
  • People who prefer digital solutions

The platform uses a modern and calm tone. It avoids heavy institutional language.

Design Process

We started by defining the main problem. Then we mapped the user journey together.

We designed the prototype focusing on the service seeker side. In the early version, therapists did not actively use the platform. Their profiles were visible to users for browsing and saving.

The design process focused on:

  • Simple navigation
  • Clear hierarchy
  • Low emotional pressure
  • Easy next steps

We continuously refined the structure based on logic and usability thinking.

What Makes Mentalink Different

Compared to existing services, Mentalink:

  • Uses a swipe based interface instead of long lists
  • Speaks in a modern and friendly tone
  • Focuses on lowering the emotional barrier
  • Combines therapist search with learning resources

The long term vision included smart recommendations based on user needs and therapist specialization.

Reflection

This project strengthened my skills in:

  • Team collaboration
  • Concept development
  • UX thinking
  • Prototyping
  • Designing for sensitive contexts

I learned that mental health services must feel:

  • Safe
  • Clear
  • Calm
  • Trustworthy

If I continued this project, I would:

  • Test the swipe concept with real users
  • Improve matching logic
  • Validate emotional impact
  • Explore recommendation systems carefully
